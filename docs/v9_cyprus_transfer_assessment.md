# V9 production-transfer assessment: Cyprus good offices–UNFICYP

**15 October 2026 | Retrospective public-source test | Baseline `ab4fe64bbefe5399771d2908a52cdfcb5c2c25f1`**

## Decision

**The V8 source-qualified, product-specific model transfers to Cyprus as a representation and quality-control method, but not as evidence for further institutional consolidation or savings.** Cyprus tests whether the proposed register would add anything to existing technical-committee support and cross-report referrals. Retain separate good-offices and peacekeeping reports, analytical responsibility, approval chains and source access. Compare the prototype with the existing joint workflow before proposing an additional register, coordinating body or system.

V9 is a second, purposively selected context—not a blind holdout, representative sample, or statistical demonstration of external validity. The source corpus had already been examined in V6. The additional work is component-level reconstruction, explicit transfer rules, discrepancy adjudication and executable controls. Neither pilot measures internal duplicated preparation. [V8 comparison](v8_shared_production_assessment.md) · [V9 summary](../data/census_v9/pilot_summary.json).

## 2. Existing cooperation is the baseline

The July 2025 UNFICYP report states that the Youth Technical Committee's inaugural meeting on **5 May** was co-facilitated by good offices and UNFICYP and refers readers to S/2025/448. The companion report records the leaders' launch on the same date and describes the technical committees operating under good-offices auspices with UNFICYP support. The launch and inaugural-meeting descriptions are linked to the same institutional milestone; actor roles, possible separate sessions and attendance are not collapsed. No new event total is claimed. [S/2025/447, para. 30, p. 7](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/447) · [S/2025/448, paras. 5 and 15–16, pp. 2–4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448).

The good-offices report also identifies the Special Representative as serving as Deputy Special Adviser. This is an observed existing leadership arrangement in that reporting cycle—not a new V9 merger proposal. Joint Contact Rooms, technical-committee support and external dialogue mechanisms already operate. V9 therefore tests **source/version controls inside existing arrangements**, not the premise that cooperation is absent. [S/2025/448, para. 39, p. 7](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448) · [S/2026/8, para. 20 and footnote 1, p. 6](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/8).

## 3. What the worked test reveals

### A. A shared number can conceal a material comparator disagreement

Both July 2025 reports give **EUR15.2 million** for 2024 Green Line trade. However, the UNFICYP report prints **EUR4.12 billion** as the Republic of Cyprus export comparator, whereas the good-offices report prints **EUR2.14 billion**. The descriptions are not identical and the retrieved reports do not reconcile the definitions or values. V9 records an **unresolved definition/value discrepancy**, preserves both values with their attribution, and blocks a single common trade/export ratio. It neither selects a winner nor averages the denominators. [S/2025/447, para. 31, p. 7](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/447) · [S/2025/448, para. 24, p. 5](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448).

This is a concrete additional quality-control use case. It is not proof that internal reviewers lacked a control, nor proof that the proposed register reduces operational errors. Resolution requires the underlying statistical definitions, vintage and reporting owner.

### B. Planned work, actual commencement and completion must travel separately

The existing **Agios Dometios/Metehan crossing enlargement** was expected to start in September 2025. The January 2026 pair subsequently records commencement in early December 2025; good offices envisaged completion before the end of January 2026. The July 2026 good-offices report describes an event marking completion in February. V9 records the February **completion-marking event**, not an invented exact day of physical completion. [S/2025/447, para. 35, p. 8](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/447) · [S/2025/448, para. 18, p. 4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448) · [S/2026/8, para. 30, p. 8](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/8) · [S/2026/9, para. 19, p. 3](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/9) · [S/2026/551, para. 19, p. 4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/551).

The enlargement of that existing crossing is **not** the opening of additional crossing points. In July 2026 the latter still appears among unimplemented initiatives. The data model preserves separate assets, scopes and status histories rather than spreading one completion label across the broader trust-building package. [S/2026/551, para. 32, p. 5](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/551).

### C. A completed initial commitment does not make an expanded portfolio complete

The July 2025 good-offices report records approval of **30 cemetery sites** and commencement of implementation. January 2026 reports completion of the agreed cemetery initiative. July 2026 describes approximately **70 cemeteries** in a larger portfolio with completed, ongoing or preparatory work. These are different scope/stage observations, not evidence that 70 sites were completed, nor a basis to sum 30 and 70. [S/2025/448, para. 17, p. 4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448) · [S/2026/9, para. 17, p. 3](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/9) · [S/2026/551, para. 20, p. 4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/551).

### D. Later reciprocal referrals show complementary reporting, not an identical product

S/2026/550, paragraph 32, describes a March digital-literacy workshop led by the Humanitarian Matters committee with Youth Committee support and cites S/2026/551. The companion's paragraph 25 describes the intergenerational technology workshop, using the label Humanitarian Affairs. V9 retains both source labels and each committee's role; it does not invent an exact event day or participant count. In the opposite direction, S/2026/551 paragraph 12 refers readers to UNFICYP for buffer-zone details. A cycle-level referral graph can consequently contain a loop without implying circular factual proof or the same drafting task. [S/2026/550, p. 7](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/550) · [S/2026/551, pp. 3–4](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/551).

### E. Units, financing perimeters and attribution limit what can be combined

UNFICYP's **297 activities including 46 intercommunal events** and the Deputy Special Adviser's **95 meetings** are not interchangeable with **over 130 technical-committee meetings**, committee membership or attendance. No record-level overlap or aggregate deduplication is inferred. Eighteen participating political parties in the Slovakia-facilitated dialogue are neither 18 people nor 18 events, and the UN is not relabelled as sole convenor. [S/2025/447, paras. 27 and 32](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/447) · [S/2025/448, paras. 11, 14 and 16](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448).

Support-facility utilization likewise needs its denominator. The selected statements report approximately **74% of EUR1 million** as of June 2025, **86%** of an allocation not restated in the selected November 2025 statement, and **67% of EUR1.5 million allocated for 2023–2026** as of June 2026. V9 does not fill the unknown middle denominator from a prior observation or interpret the final lower percentage as deteriorating efficiency. Neither utilization nor a gross allocation is avoidable expenditure. [S/2025/448, para. 20](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2025/448) · [S/2026/9, para. 22](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/9) · [S/2026/551, para. 29](https://docs.un.org/en/S/2026/551).

Leaders' written annex submissions remain attributed party statements. Their inclusion in a Secretary-General report does not convert them into Secretariat findings or agreed common facts. V9 indexes the July 2025 annex headings but does not certify every annex assertion. Separate political assessments and peacekeeping operational material remain owner-specific.

## 4. Transfer result relative to V8

| Test | UNOWAS–UNOCA V8 | Cyprus V9 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared factual inputs | Joint regional activities and explicit referrals | Joint committee support, chronology and explicit referrals | Source-linked component model transfers. |
| Differences that must remain visible | Visit dates, actor roles, announcements and later updates | Conflicting statistical comparators, lifecycle stages, expanded scope and party attribution | Qualification controls transfer; new failure modes are required. |
| Institutional starting point | Joint activity between distinct regional offices | Existing shared leadership role and supported committee structure | Do not prescribe a new joint body or assume duplicated coordination. |
| Product separation | Two reports and two public briefings | Eight reports in four pairs | Report separation transfers; briefing transfer not tested. |
| Timing | Explicitly uncertain historical replay proxies | Cycle snapshots; no exact historical availability claim | Temporal safeguard transfers with a stricter stated limit. |
| Incremental benefit or financial substitution | Not measured | Not measured | Neither case substantiates avoided work, posts or cash. |

The inference is **bounded structural portability**, not operational scalability. Eight files are not eight independent experiments; four cycles share institutions and subject matter. No cross-case success percentage or population effect is estimated.

## 5. Proposed controlled workflow and ownership

Use existing approved storage. For a demonstrated gap, add canonical component identifiers, exact source locators, units, reporting cutoff, implementation stage, scope/version, attribution class, conflict status and the responsible factual reviewer. Retain separate selected views and approvals for each report. Companion context is visibly labelled as context, not silently inserted into the published contribution.

The proposed factual custodian is function-specific: good offices for negotiation chronology and political-process inputs; UNFICYP for operational observations and patrol/incident definitions; existing committee/UNDP mechanisms for project implementation and facility figures. This is a **proposed allocation for owner review**, not an assertion of accepted delegation. Joint review is appropriate for a genuinely shared fact or unresolved comparator; it is not a new common clearance for both final products. Restricted operational records and confidential mediation material remain outside the public prototype.

The [worked support sheets](v9_worked_cyprus_cycle.md) and [component matrix](../data/census_v9/component_matrix.csv) implement these rules. The [measurement plan](../data/census_v9/measurement_plan.csv) retains V8's definitions for traceability, qualification fidelity, timeliness, net capacity and overhead. The workload template is empty. A live shadow process would measure fidelity and extra maintenance; it would not, by itself, prove that normal work was replaced.

## 6. Decision gates, financial boundary and validation

First ask the workflow owners whether equivalent version/attribution controls already exist and identify a precise missing step. Stop if the prototype only duplicates them. An authorized shadow cycle would then compare the same defined claim population, retain failures and unresolved cases, and assess deadlines, maintenance, restricted-data handling and separate clearances. Operational tolerances must be agreed before comparison. A later substitution test—not just parallel shadow work—would be required for avoided-work claims.

No savings, staff-hours reduction, new redundant post or change to a mandated product is asserted. C02 and C05 are linked to **one Cyprus test, SP09P1**, not additive cost pools. The V8 C09 regional-partnership case remains a distinct existing example; Cyprus is not evidence to promote it. C16 receives no new administrative-saving conclusion. All 14 parent ranks, mission rankings, prior data, and 20/32 retrospective output-category support remain unchanged. [Updated consolidation brief](v9_consolidation_brief.md).
