# Phase 5 — Transaction-level consolidation control audit
**As of 8 October 2026 | Public source review completed; full ledger-level validation remains blocked by source access.**

## Bottom line

**None of the three cases has been independently reconciled to paired operational transactions.** The source set contains appropriations, proposed budgets, management statements about services already transferred, a staffing-model projection, and public procurement notices; it does **not** contain the signed inventories, Umoja documents, two-sided AR/AP entries, settlement journals, actual full-service supplier costs, or service-line allocation detail needed to certify additional avoidable expense.

The 2027 SPM budgets already embed **US$1.8381m** of *projected* efficiency across these two operational cases: **UNIFIL–UNSCOL assets US$438,100** and **BINUH–UNSOH aviation US$1,400,000**. This **is not realized cash savings**, and it cannot be included a second time as an opportunity generated by this audit. **RSCE's US$3.2591m SPM 2027 allocation is financing, not a third efficiency figure.**

## 1. UNIFIL → Office of UN Special Coordinator for Lebanon

**Established from proposals:** Add.4 pp.7–11, including table 3, explicitly plans to transfer **four light patrol vehicles and two armored vehicles**, computers/accessories and rations to UNSCOL, avoiding some planned procurement. The proposed 2027 efficiency entries are **$394.0k vehicles (EFF019)**, **$36.9k ICT (EFF026)**, and **$7.2k rations (EFF029)**. Their sum is **$438.1k**, already embedded in the 2027 calendar-year proposal. There is no reason to add a separate $438.1k 'transfer' row on top of these three components.

**Independently confirmed context, not transfer evidence:** [UNGM EOIUNIFIL22026](https://www.ungm.org/Public/Notice/217790), dated October 2023, solicited market interest in **joint UNIFIL–UNSCOL vehicle washing**. This demonstrates that joint procurement requirements have existed historically. It is **neither a contract award nor evidence that any of the six vehicles has physically transferred in 2026**.

**Transaction evidence missing:** UNIFIL asset release, serial/VIN identifiers, UNSCOL signed acceptance, both ledger entries, any canceled UNSCOL purchase requisition/PO, residual liability, freight, security adaptations and installation. For rations, inspect stock lots, expiry, signed dispatch, goods receipts and valuation. Matching physical units and dates is essential: an asset may transfer with no net system-level reduction if repair and support costs outweigh the avoided purchase.

**Audit verdict:** Planned financial benefit **supported at proposal level**; implementation and net savings **not verified**. The GA's [UNIFIL financing resolution A/RES/80/282](https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/80/282), paras. 12–15, imposes safety, separation-cost, site closure and environmental controls during transition.

## 2. BINUH ↔ United Nations Support Office in Haiti

**Implementation evidence:** the Secretary-General's [S/2026/325](https://www.un.org/sexualviolenceinconflict/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/report/united-nations-integrated-office-in-haiti-report-of-the-secretary-general/8788fc76-904c-49e0-abe6-6bab89bcf2de.pdf), paragraph 40, reports that **UNSOH assumed BINUH's standard range of mission-support services on a cost-recovery basis as of 1 February 2026**. The 2027 detailed [BINUH budget Add.4](https://docs.un.org/en/A/81/6%20(Sect.%203)/Add.4), pp.107–109, independently repeats the handover, includes post reductions attributable both to support transfer and the discontinued policing mandate, and identifies the costs.

**The $1.4m and $4.0m are not equivalent services.** In BINUH's calendar-year table, aviation expenditure provisions fall from **$3.0m (2026 approved)** to **$1.6m (2027 proposed)**, a pre-booked **$1.4m** reduction attributed to using UNSOH aviation on cost recovery. At the same time, a **$4.0m** provision is made for **the standard range of UNSOH mission-support services**, increasing BINUH's other supplies, services and equipment line from **$0.7391m** to **$4.5355m** (net +**$3.7964m** including other offsets). Therefore:
- Do **not** net $1.4m aviation against all $4m reimbursement, since service scopes differ.
- Do **not** treat the $4m cost recovery as whole-UN revenue or a benefit: it is an **internal payer cost / provider recovery** until proven otherwise.
- Do **not** count reduction in BINUH staff due to its revised policing mandate as evidence of support-service duplication.

**Transaction test:** monthly BINUH payable = UNSOH receivable with matching charge ID, billable services, monthly service period and amount; then trace settlement, external aviation supplier payments, actual flying hours and surviving BINUH support costs. A match of internal bills *alone* does not prove a reduction in combined UN cash spending.

**Audit verdict:** Service handover **reported implemented**; financial charges, provider costs, service outcomes and additional addressable costs **not transaction-verified**.

## 3. RSCE: service allocation and workforce reconciliation

**Funding layers are not interchangeable:**
- [Add.1 chapeau](https://docs.un.org/en/A/81/6%20(Sect.%203)/Add.1), pp.13–15 and p.52 table 7, includes **$3.2591m proposed for calendar-year 2027** as the SPM's share of RSCE's 2026/27 activities (versus **$2.323m** approved 2026 share). The proposed change is +**$0.9361m**, not a saving.
- [A/C.5/80/20](https://docs.un.org/en/A/C.5/80/20), Annex p.3, identifies **$45.9116m approved 2026/27 RSCE gross resources** and a **$3.4432m mixed-client provision**, which also relates to special political missions and other observers and entities financed under Section 5. It **does not have the same population or annual period** as the 2027-only SPM share. Subtracting $3.2591m from $3.4432m cannot demonstrate $184.1k of double billing.
- Add.1 p.52 describes a **7.17%** 2026/27 share for SPM clients. Dividing $3.2591m by the **whole** $45.9116m RSCE budget gives approximately **7.10%**. The denominator may be an eligible allocation base rather than gross budget; obtain the approved client-rate worksheet before asserting an error or overcharge.

**Organizational staffing is similarly nonadditive.** [A/80/611](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4106033), paras.54–56 p.18, forecasts **−25 payroll-related transaction FTE**, **−10 other service-line FTE**, and **+44 transactional FTE** from former Kuwait Joint Support Office clients: a **net +9 FTE** in the *modeled transactional workforce*. [A/80/643](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4109171), paras.195–196 p.43, separately describes **82 client-location posts proposed abolished** (73 in SPMs, nine in peacekeeping entities), **11 additional proposed abolishments** in UNMIK/UNFICYP, **44 RSCE transactional posts plus one management post**, and **17 existing RSCE payroll posts transferred to DOS**. The latter are **reassignments, not automatically eliminated posts**. See [workforce source register](../data/rsce_workforce_model_vs_post_actions.csv).

**Transaction test:** actual share bills must match each client's debit to RSCE's credit, use an approved service period, and reconcile to the correct **service-line cost pool**. Separately verify unique actual payroll IDs, staff separations/transfers, and service volumes. Check whether the former Kuwait service operation stopped paying the same functions. The [RSCE finance-control description](https://rsce.unmissions.org/en/financial-services-and-compliance-monitoring-section) reports systematic transaction reviews, but their underlying reports and client vouchers are **not publicly available in this study**.

**Audit verdict:** The shared-service rationale and forecast staffing design are documented. **No client-level duplicate charge, overbilling, or incremental saving has been demonstrated.**

## 4. What counts as a transaction-level verification?

The public baseline deliberately has **zero** transaction-verification rows. The [18 prioritized ledger evidence requests](../data/transaction_evidence_requests.csv) specify accountable custodians and minimum record identifiers. Auditors need at least:

1. **For assets:** signed sender/receiver records with matching serial/stock-lot and quantities; linked Umoja GL and purchase requisition; proof the purchase really would have occurred absent transfer; all replacement, freight, adaptation and safety expenses.
2. **For cost recovery:** same-service, same-period BINUH payable and UNSOH receivable, settlement or journal clearing, vendor contracts, flight hours and billable unit rates.
3. **For RSCE:** dated approved client/denominator worksheet, client and centre matching postings, archived Kuwait Joint Support Office balances, actual staffing IDs, and transaction-volume performance by service line.

### Reproducible matching engine (local and restricted)

The [base-R transaction reconciliation script](../scripts/audit_transaction_ledger.R) checks four one-to-one transaction types: **asset releases versus receipts**, **client payables versus provider receivables**, **client payments versus provider settlements**, and **RSCE client debit versus credit allocations**. Keys include object ID and service-period dates; it rejects duplicate record IDs, invalid amounts/dates, mismatched values, missing counterparts and ambiguous many-to-one events. Its output includes **no automatically estimated net savings**, even when ledgers match. Independent review of the counterfactual remains mandatory.

```bash
Rscript scripts/audit_transaction_ledger.R /SECURE_LOCATION/ledger.csv /SECURE_LOCATION/audit-results
```

The [synthetic example](../tests/fixtures/transaction_ledger_SYNTHETIC.csv) and [engineering acceptance test](../tests/check_transaction_engine.R) contain no genuine UN transactions. GitHub CI exercises the example; a successful test **must not be represented as a UN audit verification**. Finance records, identifiable staff information, operational-security details and contract invoices should **not be committed to a publicly accessible GitHub repository**. The project's `.gitignore` blocks standard restricted directories.

## 5. Public-record disclosure limitations

The [UN Procurement Division's published awards](https://www.un.org/Depts/ptd/contract-awards/hq-Contracts/pdc017716) are **not a complete payment register**. Its public policy excludes purchase orders placed under established contracts, and contract award values are not-to-exceed ceilings, not payments. The [Umoja finance FAQ](https://umoja.un.org/en/faq) describes the internal PO / goods receipt / invoice matching process required to validate purchasing. Lack of a public procurement award cannot be treated as evidence of no contract, and a published solicitation is not an executed service or invoice.
