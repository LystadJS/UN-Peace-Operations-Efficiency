# 2025–2026 training attendance and field-activity census extension

**Source cutoff 13 October 2026 | Public UN evidence | Mission, UNITAR, UNSSC and UN Peacekeeping archives**

## Bottom line

**Thirty official-source attendance observations have been reconciled to existing or newly assigned activity identities.** They comprise **22 previously unrecorded activity or programme-group identifiers** and **eight attendance enrichments of existing EV/CJ/XEV records**. Of the 22 new identities, **three represent multi-course or multi-month programmes**, not single events; 19 have individual activity/course grain. The source publications report **22 numeric attendance counts, four approximate counts and four combined/ambiguous counts**. No individual rosters, mutually exclusive people denominator, duplicate paid service or incremental net financial savings have been established.

The census is **systematic at the bounded public source-index level, not an exhaustive census of delivered training**. [Full attendance claims](../data/census_v4/attendance_evidence.csv), [normalized participant count controls](../data/census_v4/attendance_normalized_controls.csv), [identity crosswalk](../data/census_v4/reconciled_event_identity.csv).

## Correcting document-year, programme and financial ambiguities

1. **UNSSC–DPO WPS programme, 60 staff:** The source's original dates are **22–26 August 2022**. Its undated appearance on a later UNSSC thematic page cannot make it a 2025–2026 delivered event. The [exception ledger](../data/census_v4/attendance_exclusions_and_aggregates.csv) preserves that exclusion. Conversely, the **27-trainee** DPO Protection of Civilians analytics course ran **12 February–21 March 2025**, resolving the previously undated v3 aggregate control.
2. **UNITAR second-quarter 2025 report:** The graphic presents **15 events and 504 trainees** across the whole Division for Peace, but its internal subcaption refers to *Quarter I*. This is flagged as a **source-period inconsistency** rather than silently converting its denominator into 2025 Q2 peacekeeping attendance. Similarly, 2025 Q1's **21 events and 1,593 trainees** refer to the whole Division for Peace, and selected course cohorts may be included.
3. **UNITAR's DPO training contribution:** Its [Q2 2025 report, pp. 12–13](https://unitar.org/sites/default/files/media/file/Quarterly%20Report%202_2025_Offical.pdf) identifies **DPO as content leader/standard setter** for core predeployment and women police command courses, while UNITAR supports learning design and pedagogy. The report does not show that each Seoul/Brindisi catalogue listing represents a separately completed course. The nearby **68 participants** graphic cannot be reliably attributed to a specific panel from text extraction alone and remains unassigned.
4. **MINUSCA and MONUSCO parallel courses:** Attendance for two distinct Bangui workshops in November 2025 (previous XEV009 and XEV010) and two simultaneous Beni courses (new NEV003/004) is **combined**, not a per-course number. They remain separate substantive outcomes even if common logistics could be procured jointly.
5. **MONUSCO vocational funding:** The previously publicized **$1.2 million** Muda Wa Amani II funding covers a **multicomponent reintegration project**; it is not a verified invoice total for one course, cost per beneficiary, or independently avoidable saving.
6. **Training materials versus delivery:** UNSSC's 2026 free [Peacekeeping-Intelligence Training Campus](https://www.unssc.org/courses/peacekeeping-intelligence-training-campus) advertises self-paced modules. Catalogued 33-hour material, free tuition, and availability for certificates after successful completion do **not** establish enrolment, completed learners, or department expenditure. Similarly, UNSSC's four-day customized AI course for **50 DPO Integrated Training Service staff** is described as delivered but lacks a verifiable event year on its thematic page; it is excluded from the dated study cohort.

All source-period and denominator qualifications are machine-readable in [attendance exclusions and aggregates](../data/census_v4/attendance_exclusions_and_aggregates.csv). Status labels distinguish delivered, underway, advertised only, source-period uncertain, completed certificates, and previously recorded events. Some original official pages could only be inspected through their indexed excerpts because direct article retrieval returned HTTP 403. Those limits are explicit in each attendance record.

## Shared administration and procurement reassessment

The [12-case implementation register](../data/census_v4/shared_administration_procurement_reassessment.csv) retains the previous 14-parent consolidation ranking without promoting selected field activities to statistically established redundancy.

- **Curriculum governance and core training:** DPO ITS retains standards and course-design authority, UNITAR/UNSSC supply learning delivery/methods where appropriate. Shared instructor and curriculum IDs are plausible, and A/RES/80/306 has **already authorized UNSSC–UNITAR institutional integration into UNICLI**, targeted from January 2027. No additional merger can be counted as a newly invented efficiency.
- **Joint procurement:** MONUSCO–UNDP's Bunia workshop was actually jointly financed, and MONUSCO's Beni ballistics/cyber courses shared time and reporting context. It is reasonable to investigate one event purchase order, venue, interpretation and travel procurement system, **not to assume a double charge** or substitute the technical curricula.
- **Repeated police/justice and civilian courses:** UNMISS, MONUSCO and UNSMIL audiences differ by mandate, location, recipient and learning outcome. Generic modules, assessments and attendance administration may be reusable; police investigations, victim protection, munitions safety, human rights and tactical readiness are **not interchangeable final deliverables**.
- **Community digital skills and source analytics:** UNIFIL and MONUSCO ran different local digital-literacy programmes; UNSSC and DPPA/DPO share some general data tools. Open, translated course templates or procurement could be reusable, subject to distinct civilian populations and classified data safeguards.
- **Co-financing and grant controls:** Source-stated project funding and partner contributions cannot be equated with actual cost avoided. Shared services require unique purchase orders, receiving entities, invoice/charge identities, unit costs, contract cancellation liabilities, safety requirements and independent outcome verification before monetization.

## Reproducibility and completion boundary

The [deeper archive collector](../scripts/collect_deeper_archive_frame_v4.py) snapshots up to **eight listing pages per source**, preserving original response statuses and page hashes. The [attendance evidence register](../data/census_v4/attendance_evidence.csv) and [30-row identity bridge](../data/census_v4/reconciled_event_identity.csv) tie older XEV/CJ/EV records to 22 new identities while treating three programme-group identities as **not unique sessions**. The [nonadditive source-population bridge](../data/census_v4/source_coverage_and_nonadditivity.csv) preserves old and new index/record denominators.

Run `python3 scripts/validate_census_v4_attendance.py` to confirm source-frame counts, new/prior IDs, numeric/approximate/shared attendance, calendar/date exclusions, original 2027 research comparisons and the absence of unsupported paid-duplicate claims. The [source-linked executive brief](census_2025_2026_attendance_executive.md) is suitable for project decision-makers.

**Completed in this phase:** deeper bounded official archive pagination, 30 published attendance claims, 22 newly assigned documentary identities, eight prior canonical enrichments, specific programme-aggregate corrections and 12 priority research tests.

**Not complete:** an enumerated population of all training/field activities, attendance/completion rosters for every class, signed partner allocations, vendor invoices, common-cost denominators, or independent net savings. This project continues to preserve distinct legally mandated outputs, service quality and safety.
