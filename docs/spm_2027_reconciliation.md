## 2027 embedded efficiencies: $14.6925m, not incremental new savings

The three addenda explicitly identify **31** initiative rows totaling **$14.6925m of estimated 2027 efficiencies** (Add.2 p.8 $0.0686m, Add.3 p.7 $0.0635m, Add.4 pp.9–11 $14.5604m). They include existing planned workforce adjustments, fleet and procurement changes, contracting and shared-service arrangements. Some initiatives have earlier realized gains or one-time investments; the proposed 2027 efficiencies should not be simply added to 2025 savings or booked twice as new reform benefits.

Examples with direct financial attribution:

| 2027 initiative already reflected in proposal | US$m | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| UNAMA — 61 post abolishments / two downgrades | 6.4899 | Add.4 Table 3 p.9 |
| UNSMIL — Tripoli compound arrangements | 2.6300 | Add.4 Table 3 p.9 |
| Colombia — fixed-wing aviation discontinuation | 1.6057 | Add.4 Table 3 p.10 |
| BINUH — using UNSOH aviation cost recovery | 1.4000 | Add.4 Table 3 p.10 |
| UNSCOL — transfers of vehicles, ICT and rations from UNIFIL | 0.4381 | Add.4 Table 3 pp.9–10 |

**UNIFIL→UNSCOL:** 4 light patrol vehicles, 2 armored vehicles, plus ICT and supplies. The budget credits $394.0k for vehicles, $36.9k for ICT and $7.2k for rations. Before treating any further opportunity as real, validate asset identifiers, residual procurement, transfer agreements, security requirements and whether projected cost avoidance materializes.

**BINUH→UNSOH:** the Add.4 proposed $1.4m annual aviation efficiency is counterbalanced by additional support arrangements, including an estimated **$4m** cost-recovery provision discussed in Add.1 Table 5. A transfer between budgets cannot be counted as whole-of-UN savings absent the net receiving-entity cost. UNSOH's **US$375.7688m approved maintenance** in A/C.5/80/20 is for **July 2026–June 2027**, not the same fiscal period as BINUH's calendar-2027 request.

## Expenditure across financing streams — what is and is not comparable

The **2027 regular-budget mission requests ($419.4119m)** and the **2027 proposed RSCE share ($3.2591m)** sum to **$422.671m**. The **17 reported extrabudgetary items** sum to **$32.976673m** at their printed numerical precision, matching the chapeau's approximately **$33.0m** (Add.1 para.135 p.53). They include **voluntary contributions** and **cost-recovery payments**, not a single interchangeable appropriated fund.

For example, UNAMA expects **$18.955m cost-recovery receipts** from other entities for shared premises, security, utilities and medical services (Add.4 p.127 para.327). **These receipts are internal service payments, not operational savings**, and their collections may depend on the clients' funding. The XB inventory reports explicit amounts for 14 missions, one explicit nil projection (Cameroon–Nigeria Mixed Commission), and 21 missions without separately reported 2027 XB amounts; **non-reporting must not be converted to zero**.

The older Section 3 estimates of **$37.7001m** (narrative) and **$32.7405m** (Annex IV) are **still not identical** to the detailed itemized **$32.976673m**. The itemized figures have mixed precision (notably a $4.7m rounded figure). All three historical source values remain visible; the project does **not** silently choose a 'corrected' source without a dated accounting reconciliation.

## Integrated SPM–peacekeeping crosswalk

[**16 documented pairings**](../data/spm_2027_cross_pillar_links.csv) connect each SPM to another SPM, a UN peacekeeping mission with **GA-approved 2026/27 maintenance resources**, or existing shared-service structures. Each row specifies source locations, overlap classification, historical or estimated budget scope, and an operational adjudication test.

The strongest immediate areas for cost-accounting verification are:
1. **UNIFIL–UNSCOL** physical handover, transport, ICT and rations — already budgeted transfer benefits.
2. **BINUH–UNSOH** service-level arrangements, aviation, administrative transfers and actual cost-recovery charges.
3. **RSCE–Kuwait Joint Support Office clients** — an already described shared-service transition, with risk of adding RSCE's separately approved cost and SPM allocated share twice.
4. **UNMHA→Yemen Special Envoy** residual mandate, staff and aviation contracts — already planned closure with successor cost.
5. **UNOWAS–Cameroon/Nigeria Mixed Commission** co-location and leadership — existing administrative commonality, not a proposed new merger.
6. **UNOWAS–UNOCA** transregional reporting, **UNAMA–Central Asia regional centre** coordinated analysis, and Lebanon 1559 reporting–UNSCOL source preparation — potential shared workflow, mandate rights preserved.

**Negative controls:** sanctions investigations should not be merged with independent delisting safeguards; peacekeeping military observation is not the same mandate as political mediation or sanctions designation. Common geography alone is insufficient evidence.
