# Reform obligation model — R01

The [machine-readable vocabulary](../data/reforms/obligation_schema.json) implements the [source-authority standard](source_authority_standard.md). It distinguishes an adopted GA action, Council mandate, committee recommendation, ACABQ advice, SG proposal, administrative authorization, reported implementation, and unresolved authority. These classifications are separate from candidate advancement and financial benefit classifications.

An obligation records one operative action and its conditions, addressee, entity/function scope, period, deadline and deadline source. Split provisions containing materially different actions or deadlines into separate records, preserving their common instrument reference. A session identifies the document context; it does not establish adoption. Record UN80 as a reform programme association rather than treating it as a governing organ or substituting it for the Assembly session.

The records envelope is `{"schema_version": 1, "obligations": [...]}`. Required nested fields are specified by the `*_fields` lists in the contract. Use null for unknown scalar values, an empty list when no evidence has been collected, and explicit source limitations. An unresolved record can therefore be retained without inventing an instrument, deadline, responsible owner or adopted requirement. The model distinguishes no collected evidence from evidence of failure.

For `GA_ADOPTED` and `SC_MANDATE`, the validator requires verified adoption, the appropriate issuing organ, adoption date, final-text and adoption-record receipts, an official source locator, an operative action/addressee, and a competence-review receipt. A single source may support both receipts if it actually establishes both facts; each claim must still be identified. The competence assessment must distinguish mandate authority from financing authority. Neither a budget proposal nor a Fifth Committee recommendation can satisfy the governing-adoption gate. GA recommendations remain recommendations even when the containing instrument is duly adopted.

Source receipts identify the official URL, retrieval date, precise paragraph/table locator, captured version, actual review scope and limitations. Referenced receipts must preserve the relevant quotation or paraphrase, reviewer, review date, evidence cutoff, related amendments and authentication method under F04. Where source bytes are retained, record their checksum in the source receipt; do not manufacture hashes for unretained material. A declaration passing software validation does not authenticate a receipt or its interpretation.

Implementation has its own status and operational evidence receipts, with separately identified verified and unverified components. An adoption record alone cannot verify implementation. A compliance finding additionally needs applicable adopted authority, an assessment receipt and population-coverage evidence; a finding of noncompliance requires a sourced elapsed deadline. Unknown applicability, missing records, and future due dates cannot be described as verified noncompliance. The legal/substantive reviewer must determine whether a direction or request creates an applicable obligation; the validator cannot make that judgment.

Run `python -B scripts/validate_reform_obligations.py --records PATH` to check declarations, or omit `--records` to load the vocabulary only. Run `python -B tests/check_reform_obligations.py` for synthetic negative checks. Neither command supplies institutional approval, verifies reform implementation, or supports a monetary savings finding. Other roadmap registers may retain distinct instrument and proposal schemas; they must map explicitly into this model before being represented as validated obligation records.
