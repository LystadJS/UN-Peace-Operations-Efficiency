# UN Peace Operations Efficiency — Systematic public-evidence census

**Executive readout | 8 October 2026 | United Nations Security Council reporting, meetings, field work, and training**

## Assessment

**The new evidence supports sharing factual inputs, meeting administration, policy templates and selected training resources—not reducing the number of legally mandated reports or specialist missions.** Public source identities and activities are now systematically indexed for Council reports and formal meetings, but public mission training/field-activity records are still a purposive collection.

| Evidence layer | Current verified coverage | What it does *not* establish |
|---|---:|---|
| Official Secretary-General report index, 2025/2026 through Oct. 8 | **132** report symbols: 84 in 2025; 48 in 2026 | Identical content, duplicate drafting expense |
| Formal Council meeting document variants | **474**: 296 in 2025; 178 in 2026 | 474 unique meetings — resumptions are counted separately |
| Distinct formal Council meeting IDs | **406**: 255 in 2025; 151 in 2026 snapshot | All 2026 future meetings or 115 distinct 2025 informal consultations |
| Published field/training/mission activity examples | **46** records, of which **45** occur in 2025–2026 | A complete census of all mission activities |
| Existing and added case adjudications | **36 prior** + **9 supplemental** | Independently priced redundant procurement or net savings |

The **2025 Council figure (255 formal meetings)** is independently confirmed in the official annual review, which counts **235 public and 20 private formal meetings**, plus **115 informal consultations** outside the formal meeting total. The 2026 index is an as-of snapshot.

## Highest-priority administrative opportunities

**1. Shared canonical event IDs.** UNOWAS and UNOCA jointly conducted the January 2025 Nigeria visit and February 2025 Dakar farmer–herder workshop. Maintain **one trip/event identity**, one participant/action registry and reusable factual source packets while preserving two region-specific Security Council reporting mandates.

**2. Common policy and regional briefing production.** DPPA and DPO jointly issued planning guidance and led the peace-operations review. An integrated regional ASG gives **one official Council briefing** per actual Council meeting. Centralized versioning and provenance prevent erroneous attribution of multiple outputs to a single jointly delivered product.

**3. Training materials, not training mandates.** UNRCCA and UNAMA jointly delivered an additional **28 April 2026 Preventive Diplomacy Academy session**; the **8 April 2026 Academy opening** was a different event. Regional UNOCT/UNRCCA AI–OSINT courses serve distinct national cohorts; common source materials and trainer procurement are testable, but specialist qualifications and field policing training should remain distinct.

**4. Separate political and operational reports.** The same Cyprus date can have one UNFICYP peacekeeping report and a separate Secretary-General good-offices report. MONUSCO/Great Lakes and UNIFIL/Lebanon resolution 1559 reporting likewise involves different legal tasks. Shared context is an **input-reuse question**, not a mandate-abolition justification.

**5. Preserve entity roles.** UNOWAS and UNODC coorganized a June 2026 Dakar drug-policy workshop, but UNOCA is not documented as a co-organizer. Participant, host and organizer are separate categories; an event's topic does not convert every regional UN body into a co-owner.

## Decision

**Proceed with an event–source–output registry, common reusable factual briefs, publicly releasable training modules and future administrative procurement comparisons.** Do not calculate savings using whole-office budgets, report counts, overlapping dates, or attendance. Source metadata alone cannot prove separately paid duplicate work.
